The daily loop

You run a business empire one day at a time. Each day you assign workers to jobs in your buildings, press Next Day, and read the Daily Report. Here is what happens behind that button, in order:

  1. Bankruptcy check. If your money is at −5,000 or lower, the game ends.
  2. Timed traits tick down (Sick, Pregnant, Poisoned…), then the calendar moves forward one day.
  3. Morning upkeep for each worker: max health and energy are recalculated, comfort adds joy, and joy and relationship adjustments apply.
  4. Franchises pay out, then every assigned worker does their daily stories (jobs).
  5. Income and costs are added up. Costs are building upkeep plus worker comfort.
  6. Death check, then the nightly rest: health, energy and libido regenerate.
  7. Level-ups for skills and workers.
  8. Money is applied, followed by another bankruptcy check.
  9. Governor events, then random events, then the Daily Report.
Calendar. A year has 12 months of 28 days, with 7-day weeks (Monareth, Tuelivane, Wetheris, Thurramor, Freylorn, Starrith, Sundusk). Months run from Frostveil to Nightspire.

Starting out

  • You start with $6,000 and Building 1, which you can never sell.
  • The Journal holds 16 objectives. Objectives 1–7 complete on their own. From objective 8 onward you press "Mark as Complete" yourself. Objective 15 asks for 3,000 income in a single day.
  • The story ends in an Assassination or Blackmail ending, depending on the path you chose at objective 9.

Workers

Workers come from three places:

  • Bought at the market. They want little comfort (around 1). Only bought servants can be sold, for level × 500.
  • Recruited through recruitment events. You can start one a day from the menu. They want more comfort (around 4).
  • Captured monsters, which can drop as loot from successful guild jobs.

Any worker can be fired, which pays nothing. Workers that aren't for sale can still go to a specialty buyer through the level-5 Discipline interaction (see Interactions).

How workers grow

  • Worker level: successful jobs fill a counter. At 20 × current level successes the worker levels up and the counter resets.
  • Skills grow with use. Every job adds a use to one of the skills it rolled, and enough uses raise the skill by 1. The cost in uses rises with the skill: 1 use at 0, about one more every 15 points up to 75, then it climbs steeply. Training and the Academy also add uses.
  • Each level or skill point gained lowers rebelliousness by the worker's comfort level.

Rest, regeneration and death

  • Every night: health +level +1 (+traits), energy +level + (comfort − 1) (+traits), libido +1 + level − 1 per sex job that day (never below −2).
  • The Rest job in every building gives extra energy. With auto-rest on, tired workers rest by themselves below 35% and go back to work at 95%.
  • Auto-potions: workers below 30% health or energy drink potions from stock at the start of the day.
  • Death: up to 10 graves are kept at the Church. Resurrection costs 5,000 + 1,000 × (level − 1).

Libido (NSFW mode)

  • Max libido is 20, plus traits and items.
  • Sex skills get +libido ÷ 2.
  • On non-sexual jobs, high libido is a distraction: −5 skill at libido 20, and −1 more per point above.
  • If libido would drop below 0, the overflow becomes rebelliousness.

Buying, upgrading and changing

  • Map spots: Tavern $15k, Bluehouse $20k, Redhouse $25k, Greenhouse $30k. Each spot only allows some building types; the Greenhouse takes any.
  • Upgrade from level N to N+1 costs N² × 1,000 ($1k, $4k, $9k, $16k). The max level is 5.
  • Change type for $1,000. This resets the building to level 1, 0 reputation and no skill bonus.
  • Sell a building for its purchase price − $5,000 (not Building 1).
  • Building skill (Hag Potions, Ingredients, Equipment…) = level × 10, plus up to +50 in bonuses. It is used for building checks in random events.
  • Reputation rises with good jobs (+10 critical, +5 success, −5 failure) and brings more stories per worker. Its cap is level × 200, plus your best manager's level × 200.

Daily costs

  • Building upkeep: 100 / 300 / 500 / 700 / 900 for levels 1–5 (×2 on Hard, ×3 on Nightmare).
  • Comfort: comfort level × 20 per worker on Normal (15–30 depending on difficulty).
  • Skill bonus: $100 per +10 building skill bonus.
  • There is no separate wage per worker any more; comfort is the wage.

Jobs and earnings

Each worker does at least 1 story per day. Busy buildings add more: reputation ÷ 400 extra stories per worker, up to 6. Each story rolls d100 against the worker's skill:

≤ target + 10Mediocre0
above thatFailure−5

The target is the average of the story's skills, adjusted by story difficulty, the worker's matching traits (about ±3 each), staff synergy and policies, capped at 100. Some relationship stories can't fail.

Pay comes from each story's own formula, usually 50 + skill on success and 50 + skill × 2 on a critical. Mediocre pays about the skill value. Failure costs money: the further the roll missed, the more it costs. On top of that:

  • Traits multiply pay. Each trait counts for at most ×1.2, and all traits together for at most ×1.5.
  • Business Acumen adds +10% per point.
  • Staffing: if a building's core job is empty (Prostitute, Cook, Adventurer, Bartender, Dealer), everyone in that building earns ×0.5. A staffed Expert Attendant gives the Brothel ×1.1. Each Service, Server or Entertainer worker adds +1 to every roll in their building (up to +40).
  • Difficulty and the monthly card multiply the final amount.

Managers and management skills

  • A Manager seat in a building calms it down: each manager lowers that building's random event chance by 10 points (minimum 5%). Managers also raise the building's reputation cap.
  • Your own management skills add +5 per point to the matching worker skills and stats. They are Whore Mastery (sex skills), Combat Instruction (Combat, +10 health), Servant Training (−10 rebelliousness cap), Gang Leader (+10 energy) and Business Acumen (+10% earnings).
  • Manager Level raises how many interactions you can do per day (2 + level).

Monthly conditions

From the third month, every month draws one card from a deck of 16. A card raises or lowers some skills (±10) or the earnings (×0.9–1.15) of certain buildings or jobs. The same card never comes up twice in a row, and you can switch the system off.

Franchises

Franchise Holdings are off-map businesses: $10,000 to set up, upgrades at level² × 1,000. Workers sent there leave the roster and earn about 10 + 1.5 × level + 0.75 × average skill per day.

Traits

There are 205 traits. Races (Human, Elf, Dwarf, Orc, Ogre, Demon, Angel, Goblin, Half-Breed, Slime, Centauride, Transformed) are traits too. A trait can:

  • Add or remove skill points, health, energy, libido and regeneration.
  • Multiply earnings, from ×0.5 (Reforming) up to ×1.2 (Idol, Porn Star, Transformed).
  • Set caps and minimums (e.g. Dependant caps rebelliousness at 50).
  • Change stats every day (e.g. Obedient: −1 rebelliousness per day).
  • Expire after a number of days: Sick 7, Poisoned 7, Cursed Vitality 7, Pregnant 100, Trial Worker 30.
  • Conflict with other traits. Gaining one removes the other, e.g. Rebellious vs Dependant, Obedient and Meek.

Magic traits stack: Strong Magic, Powerful Magic and Psychic all need Magical first.

Items and shops

  • Equipment: a worker can wear one weapon, one armor, one clothing and one accessory. Equipping a second item of the same type swaps the first out. Items have no durability.
  • Consumables and gifts are used up. Gifts give small boosts to joy, romance and relationship (+1 or +2 each).
  • Ingredients are for Academy alchemy. They are sold in the Basic Shop and drop from guild jobs.

Interactions

From a worker's details you can spend interactions on them. You have 2 + Manager Level interactions per day, shared by all workers (training counts too).

  • Friendship (needs relationship 25 / 50 / 75 / 80 for levels 2–5)
  • Romance (needs romance 25 / 50 / 75 / 80)
  • Discipline (needs rebelliousness below 80 / 75 / 50 / 25)

Each category has levels 1–5. To open a level, you need to have done the previous level once and to meet its stat requirement. Costs are small: a gift costs 300, a Friendly Lunch 150, and so on. They come out of your money and the worker's energy.

Training and walks

  • Training (25 skills) first asks the worker to accept: the chance comes from comfort and rebelliousness, between 10% and 98%. If they refuse, you can let it go, insist or punish. A successful lesson adds skill uses.
  • Repeat redoes the last interaction or training with the same rules.
  • Take a Walk picks a random worker and a random Friendship interaction once a day, for free.

Random events

  • At most one random event a day.
  • Normal events need a working worker. First a 30% roll, then each event rolls its own chance: 30 − 10 × managers in its building (minimum 5%).
  • Story, quest and character-arc events roll their own chance (usually 50%), and managers don't reduce it. Guaranteed and fixed-date events fire first.
  • Events have cooldowns (usually 5–30 days) and some only happen a set number of times.
  • Choices can need a worker with a skill above a threshold. The chosen worker then rolls d100 against that skill.
  • Building level boosts what events pay: money +10% per level above 1, reputation +30% per level.

The Governor

From objective 8 the Governor starts watching you, and attention grows with each objective. After the path you choose at objective 9 comes a one-time retaliation: 1–2 workers are poisoned. From objective 10 the Governor can strike any day. Strikes poison a worker, sabotage a building's skill for 3 days, or let a spy steal 5–15% of your money.
